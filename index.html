About

I am an interactive-AI researcher and educator at the University of Southern California. I build machine-learning systems that augment expert judgment and put them into daily use, and I teach students to do the same. I completed my Ph.D. in Biomedical Engineering at USC in 2026. My dissertation developed a probabilistic computational model for explainable augmented intelligence in clinical practice.

I grew up in Bangladesh, studied electrical engineering at BUET, and built medical-device prototypes before graduate school. Since then I have worked with clinicians, surgeons, engineers, designers, and business partners across USC, Shirley Ryan AbilityLab, Emory University, Virginia Tech, Arizona State University, and Johnson & Johnson MedTech. None of those problems fit inside one discipline, and that is the way I work and teach.

Every project below follows one method: embed with the experts, make their tacit judgment explicit, build the model so that it mirrors how they reason, validate it against them, and hand the result back as a product they will actually use.

xAARA: Explainable and Augmented Automated Rehabilitation Assessment

Therapists score the Action Research Arm Test by watching a stroke survivor perform nineteen tasks. xAARA scores the same tasks from multi-view video and returns, with every score, calibrated uncertainty and explanations at the task, movement-phase, and movement-quality levels. It was co-designed over a 30-month embedded study with ten occupational therapists and 107 stroke survivors. I designed the multi-camera capture system, the annotation and validation interfaces, and the database, turned the therapists' tacit judgment into an explicit vocabulary and data design, and built the engine that combines 692 calibrated models and defers uncertain cases to the clinician.

  • 94.2% task accuracy across 88 stroke survivors; 96.1% reduction in predictive uncertainty relative to single-clinician scoring.
  • Treating therapists agree with automated ratings more than 90% of the time; four external clinicians validated the system and said they would adopt it.
  • Published in ACM TOCHI (2026) and IEEE TNSRE (2024); fusion engine in preparation for npj Digital Medicine.
Published method Role Lead author With Prof. Thanassis Rikakis Venue Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence 9:1715898 (2026)

The bidirectional translator

The computational core every project here shares. A complex task is represented as a nested network whose components are partly explicit and partly tacit. A translator carries the expert's judgment into the computation and carries the computation's evidence back to the expert in a form they trust, admitting new structure only after it passes checks for compatibility, coherence, and explanatory gain, and only after the experts validate it. It is the method behind xAARA, the surgical system below, and the learning model.

DISH: AI-assisted assessment of post-operative surgical-site outcomes

A clinical decision-support system that classifies surgical-site wounds from images and assists post-surgical care teams, with patient context modeled from structured and unstructured records for more than 1,000 patients. The pilot with three surgeons runs on an annotation web portal that Academy students designed, built, and deployed under my mentorship in the research sprint I led as principal investigator. It was their first product for a real client. A pilot proposal of about $270,000 with Keck is in preparation for 2027–2028 as the step toward an R01-scale submission.

Automated quantification of upper-limb function in Veterans with neurological deficits

Aim 1 deploys xAARA during inpatient rehabilitation to track recovery in 50 Veterans with stroke, with pre-to-post assessment every two weeks across six weeks of therapy. Aim 2 extends the framework to the GRASSP assessment in 50 Veterans with cervical spinal cord injury. A computable model of reflective learning

The Academy's own pedagogy, modeled. We extracted an auditable skill vocabulary from 35 course syllabi, had faculty consolidate it into ten categories, estimated each skill's spread across domains with an ensemble of language models, and tested the resulting network with three student product teams. On that base we defined creativity within the framework and specified a computable model of the stages of learning, with the translator as its engine. For submission to Thinking Skills and Creativity. I will lead the computational implementation that follows.

Teaching

I teach the whole process, because I have done the whole process.

IDSN 543: Augmented Intelligence, designed from scratch and taught as instructor of record since spring 2025, teaches students to fit a model into a working pipeline across four cycles: image, time series, language, and video. Each cycle runs the way a real project runs. Students design the data structure, design the collection process, engineer the features, and only then build the model. They audit it against data they collected and against real-world data, evaluate it in a fit memo, deploy it as a working pipeline, and defend it in front of the class. They use AI tools throughout and are graded on what those tools cannot do for them.

Evidence

In the most recent evaluations, inclusive practice was my strongest area (3.54 of 4) and encouraging questions and discussion my highest-rated practice (3.57). Students described the course as “extremely applicable to today's world” and “my favorite academy course so far.”

Students have gone on to data-science internships and full-time roles at major companies, and several have used what they built in the course to improve their own startups.

Teaching through practice

In the 2025 Johnson & Johnson MedTech research sprint, which I ran as principal investigator, ten students chosen for transdisciplinary balance designed, built, and deployed the annotation portal now used in the surgical pilot. One served as project manager; the client's deadlines were the schedule; the portal met every requirement the stakeholders set.

I also co-designed the three-course computational module of the Human-Technology Interaction degree (Python for Machine Intelligence, Augmented Intelligence, Generative AI) and have mentored more than twenty graduate and undergraduate students.
